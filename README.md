Crop Production Analytics Using Python, SQL, NumPy & Pandas
A data-driven analysis of Indian crop production trends using Python, SQL, and Power BI.

Project Overview: 
This project focuses on analyzing large-scale agricultural data collected from Indian districts and states over multiple years.
The aim is to understand production trends, state-wise performance, and correlation between different crops using data analytics techniques.

The dataset contains detailed information about crop yields (in 1000 tons), spanning several decades and multiple crop categories such as Rice, Wheat, Maize, Sugarcane, Cotton, and Pulses.

🎯 Objectives

. To explore and clean real-world agricultural data.

. To identify top-performing crops and states across India.

. To understand yearly production trends and regional variations.

. To detect outliers, correlations, and variances between different crop productions.

. To visualize results using Matplotlib, Seaborn, and Power BI dashboards.

Format: CSV
Size: Large, multi-column dataset (districts × crops × years)
Description: Contains crop production data (in 1000 tons) for major Indian crops, categorized by state, district, and year.

🧮 Data Cleaning & Processing

. Data was preprocessed using Pandas and NumPy:

. Handled missing values using dropna() and fillna()

. Converted data types for numerical columns

. Detected and removed outliers using the Interquartile Range (IQR) method

. Computed summary statistics using describe(), mean(), and variance()

. Established relational queries using MySQL

📊 Analysis & Insights
1. Crop Trends

. Rice and Wheat dominate India’s total food grain output.

. Maize and Sugarcane production have risen steadily in southern states.

2. State-Level Highlights

. Punjab, Haryana, and Uttar Pradesh lead in Rice & Wheat.

. Maharashtra and Karnataka dominate in Sugarcane and Cotton.

3. Correlation Insights

. Rice–Wheat correlation: 0.82 (strong)

. Sugarcane–Cotton correlation: mild positive

. Maize shows weak correlation with other crops

4. Variance Insights

. High variance in Rice & Wheat — uneven production concentration.

. Low variance in Pulses — balanced distribution across states.

5. Conclusion

. Rice and Wheat remain central to India’s agriculture, but long-term sustainability requires crop diversification and modern data-driven planning.
